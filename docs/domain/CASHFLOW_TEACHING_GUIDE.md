# Teaching the Cashflow game

How an agent teaches the Cashflow game to a group of people - at a table, with friends, in any of the app's six languages. Read this first when you are asked to **explain, teach, introduce or coach** the game (not to play it: playing and analysing are in `cashflow_game_guide`). The wording to use in each language is in the manual of that language, `cashflow_manual_<code>` (`en`, `de`, `es`, `fr`, `cn`, `ar`); the numbers behind every strategy sentence are in `cashflow_card_lab`, `cashflow_strategy_lab` and `cashflow_cards`.

## The session

A teaching session has three inputs. Take them from the request; ask only for what is missing, and ask once, in one short question.

| Input              | What it decides                                            | If it is missing                                                  |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Who is playing** | tone, examples, how much to explain about money itself     | assume adults who have never played                               |
| **Style**          | how long and how deep (below)                              | ask: "30 seconds, the rules with strategy, or the full playbook?" |
| **Language**       | the language you speak and write in, and the terms you use | the language the request was written in                           |

Then start teaching at once. Do not repeat the inputs back, do not announce what you are about to do.

### Who is playing - adapt, do not lecture

- **Children or teenagers**: money as a game score, short sentences, one idea at a time, the roll of the dice as the hook. Skip loans and percentages unless asked; the goal "your passive income pays for your life" is enough.
- **Adults who have never played**: the full loop, one real example from a card, the three dangers (loans, babies, Doodads).
- **People who know finance**: skip the vocabulary of money, go straight to what is specific here - the 10% a month loan, the deposit-and-cashflow cards, why Big Deals need cash.
- **People who know the board game**: only what the app changes (it keeps the books, the cards are looked up or drawn, Undo exists), then strategy.
- **A mixed group**: teach for the least experienced, offer the depth as "ask me about ...".

### Styles

**1. "30 seconds"** - about 75 to 90 spoken words, no table, no list, no numbers beyond three. Order: the goal (earn passive income that covers your expenses), the loop (move, land, tell the app, Payday), the one trap (do not borrow for a card that does not pay, keep cash for a Doodad), the one habit to copy (read the card's deposit and monthly cashflow before every purchase). End with one sentence offering more: "Ask me about loans, the cards or strategy."

> Model, for adults who have never played: "You are stuck in a job that pays your bills and nothing else. To win, you build income that comes in without working - rented houses, a business - until it covers everything you spend each month. You move on the board, tell the app where you landed, and press Payday every time you pass it. Before you buy anything, look at two numbers on the card: what you pay up front and what it pays you every month. Do not borrow for a card that does not pay, and keep some cash for the surprise bills. That is the whole game - ask me about loans, the cards or the strategy whenever you like."

**2. "The rules with strategy"** - about five minutes spoken, 600 to 900 words. Use short paragraphs and at most two small tables. Structure:

1. The goal and how you lose (passive income against monthly expenses; negative monthly cashflow).
2. The loop (move, land, tell the app, Payday; the app keeps the books).
3. The spaces in one table: Deals (green), Doodad (red), Market (blue), Charity, Downsized and Baby (purple).
4. Small Deal and Big Deal in one example each (see the example cards below).
5. Loans: steps of 1,000 and 10% of the loan every month - an expensive bridge.
6. Doodads, Market cards and the dice cards in two sentences each.
7. The strategy in five rules (below).
8. Close with three things to ask for next.

**3. "The full playbook"** - everything, in modules, one module at a time. Say how many modules there are, deliver one, and ask whether to continue or dig deeper into it before the next. Never deliver the whole playbook in one block. The modules follow the manual:

| Module                                          | Manual sections | Add from                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| A. Goal, dashboard, professions                 | 1 to 4          | profession table: salary, expenses, passive income needed |
| B. Payday and the spaces                        | 5, 6            |                                                           |
| C. Investing: plan, buy, sell                   | 7               |                                                           |
| D. Small Deals, Big Deals                       | 8, 9            | `cashflow_card_lab` (what each is worth)                  |
| E. Dice cards and gambles                       | 10, 19          | the expected values in section 19                         |
| F. Doodads and Market cards                     | 11, 12          | `cashflow_card_lab` (what Doodads cost)                   |
| G. Charity, Downsized, Baby, bank loans         | 13, 14          |                                                           |
| H. History, Undo, saved games                   | 16              |                                                           |
| I. Strategy                                     | 17, 18, 21      | `cashflow_strategy_lab`                                   |
| J. How a game stands on the way and how it ends | 21              | progress table, bankruptcy table                          |

### Spoken or read

If the person will have the answer read aloud (voice mode, a read-aloud button, "so we can listen"), write for the ear: short sentences, no tables, no symbols (say "ten percent", not "10%"; say "one thousand", not "1,000"), no lists longer than three items, and say what a figure means instead of showing it. In every other case use what is asked for: mainly focused text, a table where a table is clearer (the spaces, a comparison of cards, the profession numbers), a real card as an example.

## What to teach: the facts to be exact about

These are the figures to quote. Take anything else from the manual of the language.

- **Win**: passive income at least the monthly expenses. **Lose**: monthly cashflow negative (the game warns, it does not stop).
- **Loop**: move on the real board, tell the app the space, press Payday when you pass it. One Payday books one month. Undo takes back any step.
- **Bank loan**: in steps of 1,000, 10% of the loan every month (120% a year). The app borrows automatically, rounded up to 1,000, when a purchase or payment is bigger than the cash.
- **Cards**: Small Deal 38, Big Deal 36, Doodad 42, Market 39. A Doodad cannot be declined and averages a little under 1,400.
- **Children**: at most three; each adds the profession's "per child" expense.
- **Charity**: pay 10% of total monthly income once, then one or two dice for three turns. **Downsized**: pay all monthly expenses once, sit out two turns.
- **A property card**: a deposit you pay and a monthly cashflow you receive after the mortgage. The question to teach is "how many months until the deposit is back, and does it pay the loan's 10% if I borrow?"
- **Professions**: fifteen (twelve printed, three custom). The caretaker needs 1,000 of passive income, the doctor 8,300.

## The strategy - five rules, in the order to teach them

1. **Know the finish line**: expenses are the target; every child and every bank loan moves it.
2. **Start small and cheap**: the Multi-Level-Marketing card is the best card in the game (about +6 points of escape chance), then single-family homes with a 2,000 deposit paying 200 to 250 a month.
3. **Move to Big Deals only with the deposit in cash.** From cash the good ones save 5 to 12 rolls; with a loan most are ruinous (minus 20 to minus 65 points). The semi-detached house with 10,000 deposit paying 1,100 a month is the exception.
4. **Borrow like a bridge, not a habit**: only for a card that pays clearly more than 10% of the borrowed money a month, and repay with the first cash.
5. **Keep a cushion for the Doodad**: 1,500 to 3,000 in cash. A Doodad is the last decision before the end in 6 of 10 bankruptcies; each 1,000 of Doodad costs 3.5 to 4.5 points of escape chance, and it costs more early than late.

Numbers to back them: a game that has 20% of its expenses covered by roll 30 almost always escapes; below 5% at roll 30 two in three still escape; the all-round strategy goes bankrupt in 16% of games; the lowest salaries escape in about seven games in ten with the best strategy, pilot, doctor and lawyer in nearly all. Never promise an outcome - these are counts of simulated games, not a prediction of one game.

## Answering questions afterwards

After the explanation stay in the conversation. The group will ask; answer in the same language and style, shorter than the explanation, and add depth only when asked ("more detail" means the next level down, not the whole manual).

- Answer from the manual and the lab topics, with the figure. If a number is not there, say you do not have it rather than estimate.
- For a question about one card, look it up (`cashflow_cards`) and, when it matters, give what it is worth (`cashflow_card_lab`): from cash, with a loan, early or late.
- For "what should I do now" with a live game: read the running game (`get_cashflow_game`) - the legal actions and the numbers - and recommend, never play for the group unless asked.
- Offer the next thing to ask after each answer: one line, three suggestions at most.

Questions people ask first, with the short answer:

| Question                          | Answer                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Do we need the board?             | Yes: you move and roll on it; the app keeps the books, the cards and the numbers.                             |
| What happens if I make a mistake? | Undo takes back any step; History lists them all.                                                             |
| Can I lose everything?            | In the game, yes - it is a game account; nothing in it is real money.                                         |
| What is the single best card?     | The Multi-Level-Marketing buy-in, then cheap single-family homes.                                             |
| Should I take a loan?             | Only for a card that pays more than 10% of the loan every month, and repay at once.                           |
| Why did my cashflow go negative?  | A baby, a loan's interest or a Doodad paid by loan; sell, repay or find a better deal before the next Payday. |
| Does the app move my token?       | No. You do; you tell the app the space.                                                                       |
| What is the Fast Track?           | The second phase of the board game; the app does not play it yet.                                             |

## What not to do

- Do not read the manual aloud. Teach from it.
- Do not give the three styles at once, and do not switch style on your own.
- Do not mix languages, and do not translate card titles yourself: use the card texts of the language (`cashflow_cards`).
- Do not state a number you did not read in the manual or the lab topics.
- Do not call the strategy a guarantee.
